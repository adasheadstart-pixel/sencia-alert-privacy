What this app does with your data

Everything stays on your phone. The app has no internet permission — it cannot send data anywhere, to us or anyone else, even if it wanted to. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics SDK, and no crash reporting service.

Reports you choose to send

The menu has a "Send a report" option, and the app offers it once after a crash. It shows you the full text first, then opens your own email app with that text as a draft addressed to us. The app does not send it: nothing leaves your phone unless you press send in your email app, and you can edit or delete any part of the draft before you do.

A report contains the app version, your phone's make and model, its Android version, the detection model and accelerator in use, the current frame rate, heat status, readiness status, whether the mount is calibrated, screen orientation, the number of trips logged with their total distance and alert count, and the error trace from the last crash if there was one. It contains no video, no location, no speed log and no details of individual trips. If you send one, we also receive your email address, as with any email.

Data the app accesses, and why

Data Why Where it goes
Camera Detecting vehicles ahead for the collision warning, and dashcam recording. Processed on-device only. Video is written to local storage (see below); no frame is ever transmitted.
Location (GPS) Speed is required to gate alerts — collision warnings are suppressed below ~15 km/h, and headway/time-to-collision figures depend on your speed. Trip distance is also computed from GPS speed. Used live, on-device, to compute speed. Trip summaries (distance, duration, alert count) are saved locally — see below. Raw location coordinates are not logged or stored.
Accelerometer Detecting hard-braking events and impacts (to protect dashcam footage automatically). Processed on-device only, never stored as raw sensor data.

Data stored on your device

  • Dashcam footage, recorded in a rolling loop and stored in the app's local storage area. You can delete clips yourself; locked clips (from an impact or a critical alert) are kept until you unlock them.
  • Trip log (distance, duration, average speed, alert count, hard-braking count) as a local file on your device. No location coordinates are included — only these summary figures.
  • Calibration settings (mount height, horizon position) and app preferences (selected model, accelerator), stored locally.
  • Benchmark and diagnostic reports, if you run the built-in throughput benchmark — written locally for your own review.
  • The error trace of the most recent crash, if the app has crashed, kept so that it can be included in a report you choose to send. A newer crash replaces it.

None of the above is uploaded, synced, or shared. Uninstalling the app removes all of it (except dashcam clips you've explicitly saved to another location, e.g. by transferring them off the device yourself).

Permissions

  • Camera — required to run at all.
  • Location — required for collision-warning alerts to function; the app runs without it, but alerts stay suppressed and the display shows "no GPS fix" rather than guessing your speed.

The manifest also declares approximate location, because Android will not show a request for precise location on its own. The app never uses an approximate fix: if you grant only "approximate", it behaves exactly as if location had been refused.

No other permissions are requested.
